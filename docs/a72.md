# The two Cortex-A72 cores

MSM8956 = 4× Cortex-A53 + 2× Cortex-A72. The postmarketOS/Nura port boots with `maxcpus=4` because "the A72 make
booting unstable". On the author's phone all six cores have been enabled at boot since 2026-11-07.

**Status: under observation.** Boots are fine (10 of 10 at the first test, every boot since). But the phone rebooted
by itself three times on 2026-11-09, with nothing in the persistent journal (it is synced every 5 s) and no ramoops
record (the RAM is cleared on reset). The A72 are the main suspect, Wi-Fi (`wcn36xx`, constant "Bad TX complete
indication") the second; nothing is proven. If you need a stable phone, keep `maxcpus=4`.

## What is set up

- Patch 0006: the A72 are **cpu4 (`cpu@100`) and cpu7 (`cpu@103`)** — cpu5 and cpu6 do not exist
  (`psci: failed to boot CPU5 (-22)`; same finding as zennnez's commit d7d0c8c). A72 OPPs 400 and 1190.4 MHz only;
  thermal trips for the big cluster 55/65 °C, critical 85 °C; the `cpu0-thermal` zone (the die is shared) throttles
  the A72 too at 65 °C; `cpu0` and `gpu` critical raised from 75 to 85 °C (under long load on a charger the phone
  used to shut down at 75 °C; Android shuts these down at 105–115 °C). Also a ramoops region.
- Patch 0007: `vdd_apc1` (the A72 rail, PM8004 S2) minimum **1.05 V**, the ceiling Android uses for 1190.4 MHz
  (`apc1_corner`, NOM group). Mainline has no CPR for this SoC, so the voltage is fixed.
- No `maxcpus` on the command line: `/etc/kernel-cmdline.d/50-device-qcom-msm8956.conf` overrides the package's one
  with a comment-only file. `panic=5` reboots instead of hanging on a panic.
- Patch 0008 (Spectre capabilities allowed on late CPUs) is only needed to bring the A72 online after booting with
  `maxcpus=4`, the earlier workaround. It is harmless otherwise.

Spectre-v2 on this firmware cannot be mitigated at all: it implements SMCCC v1.0, and
`/sys/devices/system/cpu/vulnerabilities/spectre_v2` says `Vulnerable`.

## Correction to the earlier write-up

The [earlier gist](https://gist.github.com/ryzed/76f5efeb2cdcba4d0eb74c571e775cbb) blamed the instability on the
bootloader leaving `vdd_apc1` at 0.90 V during the first quarter-second of boot. **That is wrong.** In every boot with
the A72 enabled the kernel sees `vdd_apc1` at 1.00 V at 0.18 s: the firmware itself raises the rail to 1.00 V when it
powers the cluster on. The likely cause of the old failures is that, without 0007, Linux *lowered* `vdd_apc1` to the
stock DT minimum of 0.95 V, too little for 1190 MHz. Not verified by a controlled test.

One more observation: after 11 h 45 min of uptime `vdd_apc1` read 1.00 V, below the 1.05 V minimum (five reads in a
row, cpu4 at 400 and at 1190 MHz). Not explained.

## Numbers (7-Zip benchmark, MIPS)

| | single core | all cores |
|---|---|---|
| A53 at 1.4 GHz | 1175 | 3820 (4 cores, cold) |
| A72 at 1.19 GHz | 1380 (+17 %) | — |
| 4×A53 + 2×A72, warm, after 11 min of full load | — | 3171 |

Under long full load the phone holds ~70 °C by lowering clocks (A72 at 400 MHz, A53 200–1300 MHz), peak 74 °C, no
crash. The gain is in short bursts.

## Going back

Put `maxcpus=4` back: remove `/etc/kernel-cmdline.d/50-device-qcom-msm8956.conf` and run `mkinitfs` (or edit the
`append` line in `/boot/extlinux/extlinux.conf`). If a build reboots in a loop: boot TWRP, mount the system image with
an offset (`losetup -o 1048576 /dev/block/loop0 /dev/block/mmcblk0p46`, `mount -t ext4 …`) and add `maxcpus=4` to
`extlinux/extlinux.conf`.
